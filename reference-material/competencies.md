# Marcy Lab School Competencies

## Professionalism & Productivity Competencies

* **Growth Mindset:** Deriving satisfaction from growth and proactively seeking feedback and assistance to continuously improve and evolve.  
  * Indicators  
    * Views challenges as opportunities to learn rather than obstacles.  
    * Takes initiative to learn new skills and derives satisfaction from progress  
    * Actively seeks feedback on work and incorporates it into future efforts.  
    * Asks for help when stuck rather than struggling in silence.  
    * Responds positively to constructive criticism.  
    * Reflects on mistakes to understand root causes and prevent recurrence (self-awareness).  
    * Celebrates progress and small wins along the learning journey.  
  * Pitfalls: becoming defensive when receiving feedback, avoiding challenges, giving up quickly when faced with difficulty, viewing abilities as fixed rather than developable.

* **Time & Task Management:** Prioritizing workloads effectively to consistently meet deadlines and deliver reliable results.  
  * Indicators  
    * Breaks large projects into manageable tasks with realistic timelines.  
    * Prioritizes tasks based on urgency and importance.  
    * Consistently meets deadlines for assignments and projects.  
    * Balances multiple responsibilities without compromising quality.  
    * Allocates sufficient time for planning, execution, and review.  
    * Uses tools and systems to track tasks and deadlines.  
  * Pitfalls: consistently missing deadlines, last-minute rushing, difficulty prioritizing, taking on too much without realistic planning.

* **Learning & Retaining Information:** Effectively learning new skills and retaining that knowledge. Taking ownership of one's educational journey through disciplined study and self-management.  
  * Indicators  
    * Applies concepts learned in previous sessions to new problems.  
    * Creates personal notes, summaries, or study materials to reinforce learning.  
    * Reviews and practices material regularly rather than cramming.  
    * Can recall and explain previously covered concepts when needed.  
    * Identifies personal learning strategies that work best for them.  
    * Seeks out additional resources to deepen understanding.  
* Pitfalls: repeatedly asking the same questions, struggling to apply previously learned concepts, lack of study routine, relying solely on in-class time for learning.

* **Timely and Transparent Communication:** Managing professional obligations through timely, transparent updates regarding status and responsibilities.  
  * Indicators  
    * Responds to messages and emails within expected timeframes.  
    * Proactively communicates when facing challenges or delays.  
    * Provides clear updates on progress and blockers.  
    * Uses appropriate communication channels for different types of updates.  
    * Follows through on commitments or communicates when adjustments are needed.  
  * Pitfalls: going silent when facing difficulties, missing deadlines without prior communication, providing vague or incomplete updates.

* **Presence & Participation:** Demonstrating reliability and commitment through high attendance and participation rates.  
  * Indicators  
    * Consistently attends all scheduled sessions and arrives on time.  
    * Actively participates in discussions and activities.  
    * Communicates proactively when absence is unavoidable.  
    * Engages fully during sessions rather than appearing distracted.  
  * Pitfalls: frequent absences or tardiness, passive presence without engagement, unexplained absences.

* **Collaboration:** Working harmoniously within a team to achieve shared objectives through mutual support.  
  * Indicators  
    * Contributes meaningfully to group discussions and projects.  
    * Listens actively to teammates' ideas and perspectives.  
    * Offers help to peers who are struggling.  
    * Handles disagreements constructively and professionally.  
    * Shares credit for successes and takes shared responsibility for challenges.  
    * Adapts communication and work style to complement team dynamics.  
  * Pitfalls: dominating group work or being passive, difficulty compromising, not pulling one's weight, creating interpersonal conflicts.

* **Leadership:** Motivating others and managing project lifecycles by leveraging team strengths and maintaining a positive, goal-oriented attitude.  
  * Indicators  
    * Takes initiative to organize team efforts and delegate tasks.  
    * Recognizes and leverages individual team members' strengths.  
    * Maintains positive energy and momentum even when facing setbacks.  
    * Facilitates decision-making and keeps projects moving forward.  
    * Provides encouragement and support to teammates.  
    * Models professionalism and work ethic for others.  
    * Demonstrates ownership and responsibility over one's own outcomes  
    * Holds self and others accountable in a supportive manner.  
  * Pitfalls: micromanaging teammates, failing to delegate, negative attitude that affects team morale, avoiding accountability or playing the victim.

* **Ethical Technology Use:** Leveraging digital tools responsibly and efficiently to solve problems and enhance productivity.  
  * Indicators  
    * Uses AI and other tools to enhance learning rather than bypass it.  
    * Applies an appropriate level of skepticism over what AI produces and takes the necessary steps to validate it.  
    * Properly attributes sources and respects intellectual property.  
    * Understands when and how to use various tools appropriately.  
    * Can explain and defend their own work, regardless of tools used.  
    * Considers privacy, security, and ethical implications of technology choices.  
    * Balances tool usage with developing fundamental skills.  
  * Pitfalls: over-reliance on AI without understanding underlying concepts, plagiarism, using tools to shortcut learning, inability to work without specific tools.

## Leadership Seminar

* **Critical Thinking:** Analyzing information objectively to make reasoned judgments and innovative decisions.  
  * Indicators  
    * Make decisions and solve problems using sound, inclusive reasoning and judgment.  
    * Gather and analyze information from a diverse set of sources and individuals to fully understand a problem.  
    * Accurately summarize and interpret data with an awareness of personal biases that may impact outcomes.  
    * Effectively communicate actions and rationale, recognizing the diverse perspectives and lived experiences of stakeholders.  
  * Pitfalls: jumping to conclusions without gathering sufficient information, ignoring contradictory evidence, failing to consider how personal biases affect interpretation, making decisions without considering impact on different stakeholders

* **Career & Self-Development:** Proactively seeking opportunities for personal growth and long-term professional advancement.  
  * Indicators:  
    * Shows an awareness of own strengths and areas for development.  
    * Identify areas for continual growth while pursuing and applying feedback.  
    * Develop plans and goals for one’s future career.  
    * Display curiosity; seek out opportunities to learn.  
    * Establish, maintain, and/or leverage relationships with people who can help one professionally.  
  * Pitfalls: waiting for opportunities to come rather than seeking them out, failing to reflect on strengths and growth areas, setting vague career goals without actionable steps, not building or maintaining professional relationships, ignoring feedback about development areas

* **Equity & Inclusion Lens:** Integrating diverse perspectives and fostering an environment where all identities are respected and valued.  
  * Indicators:  
    * Solicit and use feedback from multiple cultural perspectives to make inclusive and equity-minded decisions.  
    * Actively contribute to inclusive and equitable practices that influence individual and systemic change.  
    * Advocate for inclusion, equitable practices, justice, and empowerment for historically marginalized communities.  
    * Keep an open mind to diverse ideas and new ways of thinking.  
  * Pitfalls: assuming one perspective represents all, perpetuating exclusive practices without questioning them, dismissing concerns about equity as "politics," making decisions without considering differential impact on marginalized groups, remaining silent when witnessing exclusionary behavior

* **Storytelling:** Crafting clear, compelling narratives in writing and speech to effectively communicate complex ideas.  
  * Indicators  
    * Develop clear, well-organized written narratives that engage readers and convey key messages effectively.  
    * Adapt communication style and content to suit different audiences and contexts.  
    * Use concrete examples and vivid details to illustrate abstract concepts and make ideas memorable.  
    * Structure presentations and conversations with a clear beginning, middle, and end that guides the audience through complex information.  
  * Pitfalls: losing audience with excessive technical jargon or abstract concepts, poor organization that confuses rather than clarifies, failing to adapt tone or detail level for different audiences, including irrelevant details that distract from main message, rushing through explanations without checking for understanding

* **Curiosity:** Maintaining an active desire to learn, explore new concepts, and challenge the status quo.  
  * Indicators:  
    * Ask thoughtful questions that go beyond surface-level understanding to explore underlying principles and connections.  
    * Actively seek out learning resources and opportunities beyond assigned materials to deepen knowledge.  
    * Experiment with new approaches and technologies, even when not directly required for assignments.  
    * Challenge assumptions and explore alternative solutions rather than accepting the first answer that works.  
  * Pitfalls: accepting information at face value without deeper inquiry, only engaging with assigned materials, sticking rigidly to familiar approaches even when they're not optimal, viewing "getting it working" as the end goal rather than understanding why it works, treating mistakes as failures rather than learning opportunities

## SWE Technical Competencies

* **Technical Communication:** Clearly articulating the "what, how, and why" of technical decisions and product development to diverse audiences, from stakeholders to teammates.  
  * Indicators:  
    * Shares not just what they did, but how they did it and why it matters.  
    * Articulates technical decisions and their impact on the project timeline, performance, or user experience  
    * Uses effective analogies, diagrams, and code snippets to enhance explanations  
    * Adapts communication style based on audience (technical vs. non-technical stakeholders)  
    * Can effectively communicate in both writing and in oral presentations.  
  * Pitfalls: struggling to explain code clearly, lack of audience awareness, difficulty articulating technical decisions and tradeoffs

* **Mental Models:** Creating simplified abstractions of complex systems to focus on essential concepts, enabling faster learning and better problem-solving.  
  * Indicators  
    * Can illustrate a concept using an analogy or a diagram.  
    * Can explain a concept clearly with simplified language.  
    * Corrects misconceptions when new evidence emerges.  
    * Can communicate algorithms using pseudocode.  
    * Can identify essential vs. extraneous details when analyzing a problem.  
    * Accurately applies known solutions, data structures, and algorithms to new but similar problems.  
    * Makes informed technical decisions based on understanding of high-level tradeoffs.  
  * Pitfalls: memorizing syntax without understanding why, holding misconceptions, difficulty transferring knowledge to new situations.

* **Systems Thinking:** Analyzing how individual components and dependencies interact within a larger framework to design reliable, scalable, and maintainable software.  
  * Indicators  
    * Sees the big picture and how pieces connect (front-end, back-end, DB, APIs).  
    * Anticipates ripple effects of a change.  
    * Designs for extensibility, debug-ability, and reliability, not just “getting it to work.”  
    * Breaks large problems into smaller ones.  
    * Identifies dependencies between subtasks.  
  * Pitfalls: tunnel vision on one layer of the stack, failing to anticipate ripple effects, struggling to “zoom out.”

* **Methodical Debugging:** Applying a structured, methodical approach to identify root causes and implement comprehensive fixes rather than relying on guesswork.  
  * Indicators  
    * Debugs with a structured approach instead of randomly trying fixes. (see [Problem Solving & Debugging Process](https://www.notion.so/262b1966ed7480b5842ed3d5c8d0b189?pvs=21) )  
    * Reads error messages and test output carefully and investigates root causes.  
    * Tries multiple strategies when initial approach fails.  
    * Seeks to understand root causes rather than applying surface-level fixes.  
    * Can trace through code execution to understand program behavior.  
    * Tests solutions comprehensively, ensuring edge cases are covered.  
  * Pitfalls: frequently “guessing” at what the problem is without systematically finding the root, jumping into code without planning an approach first, ignoring error messages, not asking for help in a timely manner, giving up too quickly when the initial approach doesn’t work.

* **Detail Orientation:** Taking professional pride in producing clean, well-documented code and error-free materials that adhere to industry best practices and team standards.  
  * Indicators  
    * Reads documentation and instructions thoroughly.  
    * Writes clean, readable code that follows established style guides and coding conventions.  
    * Creates well-structured, error-free documentation, READMEs, and presentations  
    * Double-checks work before submitting for review or presentation.  
    * Proactively seeks feedback and implements learnings in subsequent work.  
    * Follows git best practices  
  * Pitfalls: Submitting code that raises numerous linting flags and doesn’t adhere to known style guides. Creating documentation, technical writing, and technical presentations that are error-filled or contain typos or technical inaccuracies. Repeating the same mistakes without incorporating feedback.
